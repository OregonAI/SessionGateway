# The speaker-turn index is read from a Recording Log's links when the worker fetches it, and no turn is dropped

Spec #123, stage 1, asks for every speaker turn of a committee hearing to be findable: who spoke, when, under which Agenda item, with a link into the Legislature's own player at that second. Ticket #125 wrote the parser (`parseRecordingLog`, pure); this ticket wires it to the worker, the store and two tools. The decisions are how a turn is stored, how it is linked, how a speaker becomes a Legislator, and what happens to a turn nobody can resolve.

## Decision

- **The worker reads a Recording Log's links when it fetches the file, and only then.** The store keeps a document's text and hashes, not its bytes (ADR-0005), and a PDF's link annotations are in the bytes. So `ingest` calls `extractPdfLinks` (`pdf-text`, `{page, url, rect}` for each link that points at an address) on the bytes it has just fetched, hands the text, the links and the Meeting's Agenda items to `parseRecordingLog`, and stores the result in the same pull as the document. A Recording Log stored before this step has no index and is fetched once more by the next pull that lists it (`recordingLogsWithoutIndex`): only its index is stored, its text and search entry stay as they were. A log that does not come is tried again by the next pull, which still finds it without an index.
- **The index is two tables (schema step 2, ADR-0048).** `speaker_turn_index` has a row for each log with what reading it came to: `indexed` (the event ID, the pages read by timecode, the turn count), `no_event_id`, or `conflicting_event_ids` (the IDs). `speaker_turn` has a row for each turn: the timecode, the second, where the second came from (`link` or `timecode`), the speaker as logged, the Legislator Code and Legislator id when the speaker resolves, the Agenda item text, its Measure id and the player link. The reader joins the Legislator's name from the Legislators it holds, as it does for votes, so a corrected name reaches every turn. Step 2 also runs `DROP VIEW IF EXISTS feed_row_state`, as the note beside the steps said the first step another ticket added should (nothing has read the view since ADR-0049). `versionOneSchema` is unchanged.
- **The Meeting's Agenda items and committee come from the store, not from the run's feed.** The documents step runs after the feed is stored, in a Full pull and in an Increment alike, and an Increment reads only the rows changed since the last run, so the feed in hand may lack the Meeting's other Agenda items and its members. `agendaItemsOfMeeting` and `committeeMembers` read what the store holds. The Legislators are read whole by both runs, so the resolver is the run's.
- **A speaker resolves to a Legislator Code only through the Meeting's committee.** A speaker line resolves when it begins with a title ("Chair", "Co-Chair", "Vice-Chair", "Rep.", "Sen.") and what follows, up to the first comma, names a member of the log's committee: its last word is the last word of the member's name (the Legislators rows and the alias file give the names, and a code that names no Legislator is no candidate), and when two members share a last name, the word before it must be the first name or its initial. Only a unique match resolves; the rule never guesses. A witness who shares a member's last name has no title and is not that member. A Legislator who is not on the committee ("Sen. Todd Nash, Senate District 29" before a House committee) does not resolve: the index could match them against every Legislator of the Session, but a surname alone then names several people, and the ticket's rule is the Meeting's committee. The line stays as logged.
- **No turn is dropped.** A turn is a line of the log the parser called a speaker, and each is stored with its text whatever the resolver says: `legislator_code` and `legislator` are null for anyone it does not resolve. A line in capitals ("REP. SCHARF DECLARES POTENTIAL CONFLICT OF INTEREST") is such a turn. The tool returns every turn, so an agent can see what was not resolved.
- **The player link is the log's own link with the turn's second.** `playerLinkOf` takes the first player link of the log whose `eventID` is the log's event ID and replaces its `startStreamAt`. The address, `clientID` and `eventID` are the log's, never written by this service: `https://olis.oregonlegislature.gov/liz/mediaplayer?clientID=4879615486&eventID=2025011038&startStreamAt=4`. The 34 logs of the fixture all use that shape (`mediaplayer?`, no slash before the query, one `clientID`). For a turn whose second came from its link, the result is that link as the log prints it.
- **A log with no event ID, or with two, has no index and says so.** The text stays stored and searchable, the tools report the gap (`speaker_turns.gap`), and the build report lists the log (`recording_log_gaps`, with the IDs it names). The pages whose links could not be matched to their lines, whose turns are read from their timecodes, are in `recording_log_pages_read_by_timecode`. The parser's own decisions (Q5 to Q7 of #125) stand.
- **The tools.** `get_document` on a Recording Log returns a `speaker_turns` field beside the text, which is as published (ADR-0002): `indexed`, `event_id`, `gap`, `conflicting_event_ids`, `turn_count`, `pages_read_by_timecode` and `turns` in media-clock order (the second, then the log's order), each with every field above. `meetings` gives each Recording Log a `speaker_turn_index` (`event_id` and `turn_count`, or null) and each Meeting `has_speaker_turn_index`. The result envelope is as it was.
- **Packages.** `pdf-text` gains `extractPdfLinks`. `recording-log` gains `legislatorOfSpeaker`, `measureOfAgendaItem` and `playerLinkOf`, still pure. `domain/speaker-turns.ts` holds the stored shapes. `database` stores and reads the index through `writer.ts` and `reader.ts`; `ingest/lib/speaker-turns.ts` is the one place that joins the parser, the store and the resolver; `mcp-server/lib/speaker-turns.ts` is the one place that shows a turn.

## Considered Options

- **Store the PDF bytes and read the links at serving time.** Rejected: the store holds no bytes, and the server reads the database and nothing else.
- **Read the links in a later step of the pull, like the Statute Links.** Rejected for a new fetch: the bytes are in hand only when the file is fetched. That is also why a log stored earlier is fetched once more.
- **Resolve a speaker by name against every Legislator of the Session.** Rejected for this ticket: two Legislators can share a surname across chambers, and the owner's rule is the alias file and the Meeting's committee. It could be added later as a second, labelled kind of resolution.
- **Build the player link from the Meeting's date or from the event ID alone.** Rejected: the `clientID` is the Legislature's, so it comes from the log.
- **Drop a turn whose speaker is a heading.** Rejected: the parser cannot tell a heading from a speaker without guessing, and a dropped turn is a gap nobody can see.

## Consequences

An agent reads a speaker's words' place in the recording and opens the Legislature's own player there; it does not read the words, which are in the audio (stage 2 of #123, ADR-0051).

The Agenda item and Measure of a turn are only as good as the parser's, which reads the feed's items instead of its text heuristic once one of them matches a line (owner decision Q5 of #125). Where the feed holds some of a Meeting's items and not all, the lines of the others are read as turns and the turns after them keep the last item the feed matched. The full feed lists a Meeting's items, but the fixture is a slice that holds only its chosen Measures' (HALNRW 2025-01-22 holds HB 3131 of five), and a log can name a Measure the feed does not (SFR 2025-03-17's log names SB 93, the feed SB 121). The seam-1 tests assert an Agenda item only for a log whose feed items are all in the fixture (SLB 2025-01-30). Whether the parser should read both is the owner's question.

The measure id of a turn is minted from the Agenda item's text (`HB 2167` is `measure-2025r1-hb2167`) and is not checked against the Measures the index holds.

Committee membership is the one the store holds at the pull, not the one on the day of the Meeting.

Seam 1's expected values for the three logs are in `test/tools/get-document-speaker-turns.test.ts`; the gaps and the page read by timecode are made-up logs in `get-document-speaker-turns-pull.test.ts`, labelled so.
