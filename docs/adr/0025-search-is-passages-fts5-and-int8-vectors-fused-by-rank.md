# Search cuts documents into passages, matches their words in FTS5 and their meaning in an int8 scan, and fuses the two by rank

ADR-0006 picks the model and says what is embedded. This ADR records how `search` (ticket #9) is built, and the calls the ticket, spec #1 and ADR-0006 left open. Where a number was measured, it says when, and the measurement was made on the committed fixture (278 documents, 1,690,459 characters) on one core of the desktop that ran the research.

**Passages.** The ingest worker cuts each document's text into passages of at most 510 tokens by the embedder's own count (512 less [CLS] and [SEP]): whole lines, as many as fit, ending where a line ends, with no overlap. A line longer than 510 tokens is cut between its words, and a single word longer than that at the longest prefix that fits. (`@huggingface/tokenizers` gives no character offsets, so a long line is cut at the words whose token counts it sums: the model's tokenizer splits at white space, so a text's tokens are the sum of its words'.) A passage is a slice of `document.text`, kept as two offsets, so what a hit shows is what the document says. The fixture gave 1,022 passages, 1,654 characters on average; at that rate the 357 M characters of 2025R1 are about 216,000. The evaluation run (#23) judges the rule.

**Keyword side.** One FTS5 row for each passage (`contentless`, so the text is not kept twice; `porter unicode61 remove_diacritics 2`). It gives two rankings. The first is the passages that hold every keyword of the question, or one of the phrases of its citations, best BM25 first. The keywords are the question's words in lower case, once each, without a short list of stop words, the one-letter words and the words of its citations, at most 24. Matching all of them is a decision: with "any of them" the generic words of a question ("must", "year", "benefits", "office") rank the long documents first. Measured 2026-10-30 on the 140,000 characters the search tests index: with "any", the SB 64 Introduced Version, the right answer to a paraphrased question about food aid, came 4th to 6th, behind SB 69's Proposed Amendment and an Exhibit of 54,000 characters that matched the question's generic words; with "all", the keyword side is empty for a paraphrase and the meaning side puts it first. The second ranking is for the keyword-only documents below and for nothing else: the passages of an agenda, a Recording Log or a witness-registration list that hold some of the keywords, best BM25 first, after those that held them all. A keyword-only document is never embedded, so a question with one word it lacks ("time-rounding wage claims", for the agenda that lists SB 397 and has no "claims") found it neither by words nor by meaning; every other document has the meaning of its passages looked at too, so it needs all the words. Giving every document the second ranking was tried (review of #9) and measured the same day on the same corpus: two of the three paraphrase targets fell behind SB 69's Exhibit 287879 and Proposed Amendment -1 (to second and sixth place), which share generic words with the questions, and the third was found by words as well as by meaning, so its hit no longer showed that the meaning alone had found it; a Testimony found by meaning alone was likewise found by both.

**Meaning side.** The question is embedded with the retrieval instruction ("Represent this sentence for searching relevant passages: "), a passage with none (the model card, note [1]); CLS pooling, then L2 normalization. A vector is stored as int8, each component times 127 and rounded, as a 384-byte BLOB under the sha256 of the passage's text and the model id, so identical Testimony is embedded once and a changed model, revision, pooling or instruction reuses nothing. The model id is `Xenova/bge-small-en-v1.5@<first 12 digits of the revision>|q8|cls|l2|i-<first 8 digits of the sha256 of the instruction>`. The server reads every vector into one `Int8Array` (with each passage's id and its document's index) and scans it exactly, in TypeScript, for each question. Measured 2026-10-30: 199,000 synthetic vectors (the estimate for 2025R1 below) take 42 ms to scan for the nearest 500 on one core, 17 ms when a filter accepts a third of the documents, and 78.8 MB for the arrays (resident memory grew 87 MB). 199,000 comes from the fixture: 940 of its 1,022 passages are of documents found by meaning, 5.93 per thousand characters of those documents, and 2025R1 has about 335 M such characters. Identical text shrinks what is stored, not what is loaded: 155 of the fixture's 940 (16%) were reused. The server reads the vectors when `search` is first called and again when the index's as-of time has changed since, which one small query tells it on every call; reading 199,000 vectors from SQLite took 1.0 s (135 ms to count them, 866 ms to read and add them; synthetic rows, measured 2026-10-30), during which the server answers nothing, because `node:sqlite` is synchronous, and memory peaks at twice the arrays, the old and the new. A document stored after the as-of time moved (a full pull stores its documents one by one) is found by its words at once and by its meaning only after the next as-of time: the increments (ADR-0005) must move the as-of time as they store, which is for the ticket that builds them.

**Fusion.** Each side gives its best 500 passages; they become documents (one per hit id, at its best passage), and reciprocal rank fusion with k=60 puts the three rankings in one: the documents that hold every keyword, the documents nearest the question in meaning, and the keyword-only documents that hold some of the keywords (`search/ranking.ts`). Ties come in that order, so a keyword-only document that shares one word with a question does not outrank a document found by meaning at the same rank, and a document that holds every word outranks both. A query with no keywords besides its citations has no meaning to look for, and an index with no vectors nothing to look in, so neither embeds the question.

**A hit** is a document, or for a Proposed Amendment the amendment (its postings are one node, ADR-0011), and carries its id (`get_document` resolves it), `document_id` (the posting whose text matched), kind, title, Measure, date, a snippet of about 300 characters from the passage that matched, `source_url`, and `found_by`: "citation", "keyword", "embedding", or several. A call returns 20 hits, or `limit` up to 50. The title is the Measure's citation and the feed's label ("SB 64 Introduced", "SB 69 Fiscal Impact Statement (A)", "SB 69-2"), an Exhibit's or Recording Log's title as published, or the committee's name and the label for an agenda. The date is the start of the meeting a document is posted to or for (an agenda, an Exhibit, a Recording Log, Testimony, a posting), else when OLIS created the row (a Version, a Printed Amendment, an analysis document): `DocumentLink.date`.

**Exact citations come first.** A citation is found anywhere in the question, in any case, with or without the space: a Measure ("HB 3131"), a Proposed Amendment ("SB 69-2", "HB 3795-A3") or an ORS section ("ORS 468.130"; a line may break after "ORS"). "First" means ahead of every other hit, in the order the question names its citations, and the filters still apply to them. Among the exact matches of one citation:
- a Measure: its Versions, from Introduced to Enrolled, by the date OLIS created each (a Printed Amendment is not a Version);
- a Proposed Amendment: the amendment, as one hit whose text is its latest posting's (the one `get_document` serves for it);
- an ORS section: the documents whose whole text names that exact section, oldest first (by the date they are filed under, then by id). The worker reads the citations of each document over its whole text when it stores it (`findCitations`, which takes any run of white space, a line break included, between "ORS" and the number) and keeps them with where the first mention starts, so "ORS" at the end of one passage and "192.245" at the start of the next is still the document's citation: matching passage by passage dropped HB 3795's and SB 69's Enrolled Versions from the exact hits of "ORS 192.245" and "ORS 279C.800", found in the review of #9. The number may not run on into another digit or letter, so "ORS 468.1305" is not "ORS 468.130" (they are two ids). A hit found this way shows the passage that holds the first mention, or the passage it starts in when a boundary cuts it. Statute Links are not here yet (#13): when they are, they are the better evidence, and this rule becomes the fallback. The worker keeps the Measure and Proposed Amendment citations a document makes too, but those are ranked by what they name (a Measure's Versions, the amendment), not by the documents that mention them.

A citation that names nothing the index holds puts nothing first, and the result's `notes` name it ("The index has no Proposed Amendment \"SB 69-3\", so no hit is an exact match for it."). The index holds a Measure when it lists it, a Proposed Amendment when a posting of it is stored, and an ORS section when a stored document names it, whatever the filters leave out: a filter that hides the documents of a citation the index holds gives no note. A citation's own words and numbers are not keywords of the question ("3" of "SB 69-3" would match the numbers in a PDF's margins): the citation is looked for as a whole, as an exact match and as its words in order, and the rest of the question is ranked as it would be without it. A hit that a citation named is shown round the citation, and when a passage boundary cuts a mention in two, the passage's text runs on far enough to hold it.

**Testimony** is left out unless the call asks (`include_testimony`), asks for the kind (`kind: "testimony"`), or names a Measure (`measure`).

**Keyword-only documents** are agendas, Recording Logs and witness-registration lists. The feed labels a witness-registration list "Witness Registration" in `DocumentType` (2,368 of the 2025R1 cache's 16,115 CommitteeMeetingDocuments rows, measured 2026-10-30), and files 45 more under "Meeting Material" (44) or "Presentation" (1) with a title that says "witness registration". (Some titles under "Witness Registration" are misspelled, "regitration" and "witeness registration" among them, which the label catches; a misspelled title under another label would not be caught, and none was found.) The rule counts a row whose label is "Witness Registration" or whose title matches `witness\s+registration`. Their passages are indexed for keywords and never embedded, and the vector scan reads only the passages of documents that are not keyword-only, so a Recording Log with the text of an Exhibit is found by its words and the Exhibit by both.

**The worker** stores a document, its passages, its FTS rows, its citations and the vectors of the passages the index does not hold in one transaction, so a document is never stored without being searchable. Embedding is one thread unless `MODEL_THREADS` says more (the server always uses one); the deploy box has two cores shared with seven corpora (ADR-0007). `build-report.json` has `search_index`: the model id and, for the documents that pull stored, the passages, the keyword-only passages, the passages embedded and the passages reused. The whole fixture took 105 s of wall time in one worker on one thread, with the PDFs read from a local fake: 785 embedded, 155 reused, 82 keyword-only; the worker's resident memory peaked at 962 MB.

**Not built.** A similarity floor (das-cairn uses one per model; it needs the evaluation set to place); re-embedding of documents already stored when the model changes (a document is fetched and indexed once, so a new model means a new database); joining a word that a PDF broke across lines with a hyphen ("appropri-ated") before indexing it; collapsing identical Testimony in the hits (the `testimony` tool, #14, counts it).

## Considered Options

- **sqlite-vec** for the scan. Rejected by the research (docs/research/embeddings-and-storage.md §2.1, §2.3): slower than a flat array at every size measured, pre-v1 and native.
- **FTS5 on whole documents.** A 300-page Version is one row that scores by its length, and a snippet needs the passage anyway.
- **FTS5 `content=` the passage table.** The text would be kept twice (357 M characters); offsets into `document.text` cost nothing.
- **A fixed number of hits per side (200).** One long document can hold dozens of the best passages, and the hits are documents; 500 passages leave room for 50.
- **"Any keyword" matching,** as above.
