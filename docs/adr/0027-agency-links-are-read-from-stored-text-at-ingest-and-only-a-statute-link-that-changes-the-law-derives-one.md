# Agency Links are read from the stored text at ingest, one document at a time, and only a Statute Link that amends, repeals or adds to derives one

ADR-0004 says what the four kinds of evidence for an Agency Link are, ADR-0012 that only the worker reads the snapshot, and ADR-0012 left to "the first ticket that reads" the registry or the Chapter table how it is kept. Ticket #16 is that ticket. This ADR records where the links are made, where they sit, and the one rule that decides what a Statute Link derives.

**Where they are made.** The extractor (`agency-links`, ticket #7) is pure and takes the registry as a parameter. The worker is the only code with the registry and the Chapter table (the snapshot file), so it makes every link, at the end of a full pull, after the files are stored and their Statute Links read (ADR-0015). It reads each stored document of three kinds that it has not read: a Fiscal Impact Statement (an analysis document whose feed label is exactly "Fiscal Impact Statement": 1,442 of the 5,260 rows of the full 2025R1 cache, counted 2026-10-30), a Version, and a posting of a Proposed Amendment. A Printed Amendment, the other analysis documents, Exhibits and the rest are not read, for the reason ADR-0015 gives for Statute Links. Fiscal Impact Statements go to `fiscalImpactAgencyLinks`, which also returns the units that are not Agencies; Versions and postings go to `textAgencyLinks`, and, with their Statute Links, to `derivedAgencyLinks`. The links are kept in `agency_link` (the Agency, the kind, and the evidence as JSON), the units in `unresolved_unit`, and `agency_read` says a document was read, and with which snapshot, so that a Version with no link is not read again at every pull and every document is read again when the snapshot changes. A file with no text layer is recorded as read and gives nothing. Testimony's links stay where ADR-0016 and ADR-0017 put them, in `testimony.agency_id`, made again at every pull from the feed's row; copying them would give two answers to refresh.

**Where a link sits.** A stored link names the document its evidence was read from, and the node it sits on is found when the server answers, as ADR-0013 places an analysis document. A Version's and a posting's links sit on the Version and on the posting's Proposed Amendment. A Fiscal Impact Statement's sit on the Version the feed's label places it on (the `version` edge, ADR-0013) when the store holds that Version's file, and otherwise on the Measure. Testimony's sit on the Measure. So a statement moves from the Measure to a Version by itself: SB 69's statement 85414 (created 2025-01-30, label "A") sits on SB 69 until the feed lists the A-Engrossed Version, which OLIS created on 2025-02-03, and on that Version from then on. The ticket says the link sits "on the Version ... and on the Measure". The link is listed once, as the Version, with the Measure it belongs to in `measure_id`; what asks about a Measure (`get_measure`, search's Agency filter) takes every link of its nodes. Listing it twice would give `find_measures` two items for one fact.

**What derives a link.** A Statute Link derives an Agency Link when it amends, repeals or adds to an ORS section or chapter, and a citation derives none. The Chapter table gives a chapter every Agency whose rules implement one of its sections, and a citation says nothing about whom a Measure is about. Chapter 192 (public records) has 57 Agencies in the fixture's snapshot (about 60 in the whole one, by the lead's count), and SB 69-2 alone cites "ORS 192.311 to 192.478" (pdftotext of proposed-amendments/26286.pdf), which the parser expands into every section of the range. Measured 2026-10-30 by letting a citation derive: the A-Engrossed Version of SB 69 had a derived link to 92 Agencies, where its changes reach 7. With citations out, question (a), "what touches statutes my Agency administers", is answered by what changes. A chapter target ("added to and made a part of ORS chapter 657B") derives from the chapter itself, and a chapter the table has no row for derives nothing.

The evidence says which link and chapter derived it. A document's links in one chapter are one piece of evidence for each Agency of the chapter, not one for each link: `chapter`, the Statute Link in the lowest measure section that reaches the chapter (the kind, the target and the measure section), and `links_in_chapter`, how many of the document's links that change the law reach it. Chapter 183 has 123 Agencies in the whole snapshot, so a row for each link and Agency would be thousands for a long Version, and the lowest measure section is something the reader can find in the text and does not depend on the order the parser emits links in. SB 69's changes reach chapter 657B four times (SECTIONS 1, 3, 6 and 7), 659A three times and 653 twice, so the Bureau of Labor and Industries has three pieces of evidence for each of its four nodes.

## Considered Options

- **Derive from every kind of Statute Link.** Rejected by the measurement above.
- **Keep the Chapter table in the database and derive when the server answers.** The links would follow a new snapshot without a rebuild. But the table would be copied into every database, the server would hold the join (`statute_link` has a row for each link, 292 for each of SB 69's A-Engrossed and Enrolled Versions), and the brief puts the work in the worker, where the registry already is. ADR-0012's reading, that the server learns nothing of the snapshot but its version, holds.
- **One row for each Statute Link and Agency.** Says every link, at the price above.
- **Put a Fiscal Impact Statement's links on the Version and on the Measure, as two rows.** Two items for one fact in `find_measures`, as above.
- **Read the Printed Amendments.** The ticket names Versions and Proposed Amendments, and ADR-0015 does not read them.

## Consequences

- A document is read once for each snapshot (fix pass of #16, F7). `agency_read` says with which commit of the OregonAI snapshot a document was read, and the worker reads again, from the stored text and without fetching, every document read with another commit, so the index never reports a snapshot (`snapshot_state`, `session_overview`) that some of its Agency Links were not made with. Before, a changed snapshot reached Testimony, which is matched at every pull (ADR-0016), and no other link: an alias the registry gained was found in a new Testimony and in no Version already read. A document's earlier links are replaced in the same transaction as its new reading. An extractor that improves still rebuilds the database, or deletes a document's `agency_read` row.
- Statute Links are not read again when the snapshot changes. Their section list (ranges are expanded against it) is the snapshot's, and the derived links are made from the Statute Links as stored with the Chapter table of the new snapshot, so a snapshot whose section list differs gives derived links from links expanded with the old one. Whether a snapshot change reads the Statute Links again is ticket #13's open question to the owner (ADR-0015); this change makes it more pressing, because Agency Links now follow the snapshot and Statute Links do not.
- A Version whose file did not come cannot be a node: a statement the feed places on it sits on the Measure until the file is stored.
- A sub-unit of an Agency is a row of its own in the registry, and the table gives a chapter the sub-units that implement it (ADR-0012): the Department of Human Services' chapters 409 and 418 name the Department and its Child Welfare Programs, Self-Sufficiency Programs and others, each its own Agency Link. A chapter the table gives only to a sub-unit also gives the Department a link, through the sub-unit (ADR-0029).
- `get_measure` does not list every link (fix pass of #16, F5 and L1). Listing them all made SB 69's answer 92,268 characters, up from 65,272 (ADR-0015), of which `agency_links` was 26,980 (82 pieces of evidence for 15 Agencies) and grew with Agencies × nodes × chapters (chapter 183 alone has 123 Agencies). Now each Agency has its slug, its parent, and its evidence grouped by kind, each kind with its count and the first three examples; each document the examples and the units name appears once in `documents`, with its `source_url`; and a link derived from a Proposed Amendment whose Statute Links `statute_links_by_source` does not list (ADR-0015) is left out and counted in `note`. Every link is in `find_measures` with the Agency, which is paged. SB 69's answer is 65,515 characters and its `agency_links` 15,171 (16 Agencies; the 24 derived links left out are those of -1 and -2). What is bounded is each Agency's entry (a kind has a count and three examples), not the number of Agencies: a Measure whose three Versions each change a section of chapter 183, which the registry gives 123 Agencies, would be about 112,000 characters of `agency_links` (measured on made-up links through the same function; before this change the same would have been about 137,000), and whether to list the Agencies beyond some number by slug and count alone is left to the owner. The same pass cut 14,944 characters from `statute_links_by_source`: a Version whose reading is exactly that of the Version before it in print order says `same_as` that Version's id (SB 69's Enrolled repeats A-Engrossed's 292 links), and a Version that was not read is never the same as another. Once the branch was rebased onto tickets #18 and #22, `same_as` compares the whole entry an agent would otherwise see: the reading, the Statutes Affected Table's block beside it (ADR-0033) and its `no_longer_listed` flag (ADR-0030), so a Version that differs from the one before it only in its block or its flag is listed in full (`get-measure-same-as-table-pull.test.ts`, `no-longer-listed-agency-links.test.ts`). The 2025R1 table names SB 69's A-Engrossed Version and no entry names an Enrolled one, so a deployment that loads the table gives A-Engrossed a block and Enrolled none, lists the Enrolled Version in full, and does not save those characters for SB 69.
